3. Keypad-aware vs. ordinary edit distance completed 2026-10-07

Fixed diagnostic matrix (n=3 per cell, 60 cases per model) · baseline: unit-cost Levenshtein, same eligibility/ranking/1.6 cutoff as the keypad check · runs/feedback/recipient_matrix.json · not a customer study — a small, fixed, transparent comparison.

The trade-off, stated plainly: keypad detection catches the single most common real typo (a digit swap) that ordinary edit distance misses entirely — but it also produces more false suggestions on legitimately similar numbers and on unfamiliar recipients. Both are reported; neither is cherry-picked.

5. Ablation & fairness

Ablation: removing a feature group and retraining costs the most PR-AUC for counterparty (−0.137), behaviour (−0.026), complaints (−0.016) — full model 0.962. Component view: LightGBM-only 0.962, +IsolationForest 0.962, +graph rules 0.962, fused (all three) 0.962 — fusion weights land entirely on LightGBM on this data; the other two still drive reason codes and the unsupervised-anomaly signal.
Fairness (groups with false-positive rate >1.25× overall, ≥1,000 legit txns): age 45–59 (NUDGE ×1.33), age 60+ (NUDGE ×1.12, STEP_UP ×1.65), farmer/rural (NUDGE ×1.99, STEP_UP ×2.22), small business (NUDGE ×1.28, STEP_UP ×1.57), KYC1 (NUDGE ×1.23, STEP_UP ×1.34). None of these attributes are model inputs; monitored in reports/fairness.csv, not corrected with per-group thresholds.
